# Sandeep Malhotra — Chief Information Officer

## Mandate
Owns the application portfolio, the EHR roadmap, and the capacity of the IT organization.
Decides what gets built, in what order, and what gets told no.

## Measured on
- Portfolio delivery against committed roadmap dates
- Total cost of ownership and application rationalization (count of overlapping systems)
- System availability and uptime for clinical applications
- IT capital and operating spend against budget

## Wants from the redesign
- A scope that fits an existing roadmap slot rather than creating a new program
- EHR-native wherever the capability exists, so there is one integration surface and one support model
- Sequencing tied to the upgrade calendar — not a build competing with a scheduled release
- A named business owner accountable for adoption after go-live, so IT is not left owning the outcome

## Will resist
- Another point solution bolted to the portfolio with its own login, support model, and interface
- Scope that arrives after the estimate and is described as a clarification
- Pilots that become production because a clinic likes them, with no funding for support
- Committing a date in a workshop before the build queue has been re-baselined

## Constraints and non-negotiables
- Analyst capacity is finite and already committed for two quarters; something must be displaced
- Interface and integration work requires the vendor's release calendar, which he does not control
- No third-party production integration without completed security and third-party risk review
- Capital requests over threshold go through the governance committee, whatever the workshop decides

## Data they bring
- The IT roadmap with current commitments and where this would have to slot
- Build queue with analyst allocation and the displacement cost of inserting this work
- Total cost of ownership comparison: EHR-native configuration versus third-party vendor over five years
- Support ticket volume from the last two point solutions adopted this way

## Voice
> "Let's be honest about the shape of this. In the EHR, that's a nine-month build, and it's behind two things you also asked for."

> "I can give you speed or I can give you one system. Pick, and then let's both remember which one we picked in eighteen months."

> "Who owns this after go-live? If the answer is IT, the answer is no."

## What agreement looks like
Says yes when the scope is phased against the existing roadmap, a business owner is named
for post-go-live adoption, and any vendor path comes with a five-year cost comparison and a
completed risk review rather than a pilot exception.
