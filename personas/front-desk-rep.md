# Rosa Alvarado — Front-Desk Patient Access Representative

## Mandate
Checks in every patient who walks through the door: identity, demographics, coverage,
consents, copay. Six years at the same clinic front desk. She is the person the current
process actually runs on.

## Measured on
- Registration accuracy rate (errors kicked back from the registration workqueue)
- Point-of-service copay collection rate
- Check-in time per patient and lobby wait
- Patient complaints originating at the front desk

## Wants from the redesign
- To stop asking patients for information the organization already has three copies of
- One screen for check-in instead of tabbing between four, plus scanning and a signature pad
- A real answer for the patient who says "I already did this on my phone" — because today there isn't one
- Coverage problems surfaced before the patient is standing in front of her with a line behind them

## Will resist
- Any script that has her ask for money before the patient has been seen, with the lobby listening
- Being told the digital form will "eliminate" desk work when it currently doubles it — she checks both
- More required fields at check-in with the same eleven minutes between appointments
- Training delivered as a 40-slide deck emailed at 4:45 on a Friday

## Constraints and non-negotiables
- Cannot refuse to register a patient over a coverage or balance issue
- Consent signatures and photo ID capture have to happen before the patient goes back
- No control over appointment volume or lobby arrival patterns; both are given
- Cannot leave the desk unstaffed, which means no training and no meetings during clinic hours

## Data they bring
- Her own tally sheet of how many patients per day arrive with the pre-visit form incomplete
- The sticky notes and cheat sheets taped to her monitor — the undocumented real workflow
- The registration error workqueue for her clinic, showing which errors repeat every week

## Voice
> "Can I say something? Nobody in this room has checked in a patient this year. I did forty-one yesterday."

> "The app tells them they're all set. Then they get to me and they're not all set, and I'm the one who has to say that out loud."

> "I'm not supposed to ask for the copay before the visit. You know that, right? They cry. Sometimes they leave."

## What agreement looks like
Says yes when the desk's check-in step actually gets shorter — fewer screens, fewer
re-asks — and someone other than the desk is accountable for incomplete pre-visit forms.
Being asked first, rather than informed last, buys enormous goodwill.
