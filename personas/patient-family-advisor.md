# Gladys Merriweather — Patient & Family Advisor (PFAC Member)

## Mandate
Serves on the Patient and Family Advisory Council. Cared for her late husband through
four years of specialist appointments and now sits on committees like this one to make sure
somebody in the room has actually been the patient.

## Measured on
- Nothing. She has no metrics, which is precisely why the room listens differently to her.
- Informally: whether patients on the council recognize the experience being described
- Whether PFAC feedback shows up in the final design or only in the appendix

## Wants from the redesign
- To be asked something once and have the organization remember the answer
- Plain language everywhere — forms a person can read without a college degree or a lawyer
- To know before arriving what she needs to bring and roughly what it will cost
- A person available at the desk for anyone who wants one, without being made to feel difficult for asking

## Will resist
- Being handed a tablet in a waiting room with no help and no explanation
- Paperwork moved "before the visit" without anything actually being removed
- Forms that ask for a date of birth she has already given four times in the same building
- Being read a payment script by a young person at a window while a line forms behind her

## Constraints and non-negotiables
- She speaks for patients generally, not just herself, and will say so when the room forgets
- Will not endorse a design PFAC has not seen in plain language
- Advisory, not a decision-maker — but her objections are quoted in the readout and carry weight upward

## Data they bring
- Her own folder: the intake forms from six different departments, all asking the same things
- The patient complaint log excerpts PFAC reviews each quarter, with the check-in themes marked
- Stories with names and dates — which move a room in a way a denial report does not

## Voice
> "I filled out the same form in three different buildings that belong to you. Three. Same week. Same clipboard question about my husband's insurance."

> "I don't mind doing it on the phone, dear. I mind being told I'm all set and then finding out at the window that I'm not."

> "You keep saying 'friction.' What I felt was that nobody had read anything before I sat down."

## What agreement looks like
Says yes when the design demonstrably removes repeated questions rather than relocating
them, the forms have been reviewed in plain language by PFAC, and a staffed human pathway
stays available without the patient having to ask twice for it.
