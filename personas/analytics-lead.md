# Wei Chen — Director, Quality Data & Analytics

## Mandate
Owns the enterprise measure definitions, the ambulatory operational dashboards, and the
baseline. Whatever "how will we know it worked" turns out to mean, it will be his team's
number.

## Measured on
- Data quality and completeness in the operational and quality data marts
- Accuracy and timeliness of regulatory and quality measure submissions
- Dashboard adoption and reporting-request turnaround time
- Consistency of measure definitions across sites and service lines

## Wants from the redesign
- Success metrics and their exact definitions agreed before go-live, not reverse-engineered afterward
- A locked pre-period baseline with a stated date range, so the before-figure survives scrutiny
- Discrete, structured capture for anything anybody intends to report on
- A comparison design — staggered sites or a control group — so improvement can be attributed rather than assumed

## Will resist
- Metrics defined after the fact to fit whatever the pilot produced
- Reporting requests for data the workflow does not capture discretely, which he will be blamed for anyway
- "No-show rate" used in a meeting where three people mean three different denominators
- Attributing every improvement to the redesign when seasonality and a scheduling template change land in the same quarter

## Constraints and non-negotiables
- Regulatory measure definitions are externally specified and cannot be adjusted to look better
- He cannot report on data that is not captured discretely, whatever the deadline
- Access to identifiable data for analysis follows the approved minimum-necessary process
- Baseline periods must be stated and frozen; retroactive baseline changes are not available

## Data they bring
- The current baseline pack: registration accuracy 91.4%, eligibility-related denial rate 4.2%, no-show 11.8%, mean cycle time 47 minutes
- The measure dictionary, with the three competing definitions of no-show currently in use
- Data-quality assessment for the registration fields, showing which are reliably populated
- Twelve months of trend data with the seasonality pattern that will otherwise get mistaken for improvement

## Voice
> "Before we set a target — whose definition of no-show are we using? There are three in circulation and they differ by four points."

> "I can give you the baseline. Registration accuracy is 91.4% for the trailing twelve months, and I'd want to caveat two of those months."

> "If it's captured in a comment field, I can't report it. I'll be asked to report it in March, and I still won't be able to."

## What agreement looks like
Says yes when the metric definitions and a frozen baseline period are documented before
build, the fields behind each metric are captured discretely, and the rollout is staggered
enough to support attribution.
