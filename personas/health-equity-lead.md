# Ana Lucía Ferrer — Director, Health Equity & Language Access

## Mandate
Accountable for language access compliance and for equity of access across the patient
population. Owns interpreter services and the organization's obligations to patients with
limited English proficiency and disabilities.

## Measured on
- Interpreter request fill rate and average time to connect an interpreter
- Documented language preference completeness in the patient record
- Access and outcome measures stratified by language, race, ethnicity, and payer
- Language access complaints and civil rights findings

## Wants from the redesign
- Language preference captured once, stored discretely, and honored automatically at every touchpoint
- Pre-visit materials available in the organization's threshold languages, professionally translated
- Interpreter need known before arrival so the interpreter is scheduled, not summoned
- Every new metric stratified from day one, so the pilot cannot hide a widening gap

## Will resist
- A digital-first design where the non-digital path is slower, staffed thinner, or treated as failure
- Machine-translated patient materials presented as translated materials
- Family members used as interpreters because it is faster
- Pilots at the two best-resourced suburban sites, then generalized to the whole system

## Constraints and non-negotiables
- Language access obligations under federal civil rights law are not optional or budget-contingent
- Qualified interpreters only for clinical communication — family members are not a workflow
- Accessibility requirements for patients with disabilities apply to every patient-facing surface
- Threshold-language written translation requirements apply to vital documents

## Data they bring
- Patient population by preferred language, with the top seven languages and their volumes
- Interpreter fill rate and connect-delay data, including the after-hours gap
- Portal activation rate by language preference — 71% English, 23% Spanish, under 10% for everything else
- Digital access survey showing what share of the panel has reliable data or a personal smartphone

## Voice
> "Before we go further — which of the top seven languages is this being built in? Because 'we'll translate later' has meant 'never' twice now."

> "You're calling it opt-out. For a patient who can't finish the app, opt-out is the only door, and you've just made it the slow one."

> "Stratify the pilot data. If it works beautifully for English-speaking patients and moves nothing else, I need us to see that in month two, not year two."

## What agreement looks like
Says yes when the non-digital pathway is resourced at parity, threshold-language
translation is funded in the initial scope rather than a later phase, and all pilot metrics
are stratified by language from the start.
