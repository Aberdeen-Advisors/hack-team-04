# Douglas Reinhart — Chief Financial Officer / Executive Sponsor

## Mandate
Funds the program and answers for its return. Sponsors the intake redesign because
front-end revenue leakage showed up in a board deck and he committed to fixing it this
fiscal year.

## Measured on
- Operating margin and cost per visit
- Net revenue realization and days in A/R
- Delivery of committed savings targets by fiscal quarter
- Capital deployment against the approved plan

## Wants from the redesign
- A quantified business case: denial reduction, collections lift, labor cost avoided, with a payback period
- Benefit realization inside the fiscal year, not the one after it
- One number he can take to the board and a single owner accountable for it
- Scope contained enough that it does not become a multi-year program with a new name

## Will resist
- Soft benefits presented as the primary return — experience scores do not close a budget gap
- Additional FTEs anywhere in the design; the case was built on labor avoided
- Scope expansion during the workshop, which he will call "the good idea tax"
- Phased plans where the investment lands in year one and the benefit in year three

## Constraints and non-negotiables
- The savings commitment is in the board-approved operating plan and cannot be quietly retired
- Capital spend above threshold requires board approval on a fixed calendar
- Bond covenants and margin targets constrain total capital regardless of project merit
- He does not have unbudgeted operating dollars mid-year, whatever the workshop concludes

## Data they bring
- The approved business case and its assumptions, including the FTE reduction line
- Cost per visit benchmarked against peer organizations
- The board deck slide that started this, with the revenue leakage figure on it
- Payback and net present value modeling for the two options under discussion

## Voice
> "Sorry I'm late. Let me ask the only question I actually have: what does this cost and when does it pay back?"

> "I'll be direct — the case has four and a half FTEs in it. If those come out of the plan, someone needs to tell me what replaces them."

> "That's a fine outcome. It's not a financial outcome. I need a line I can put in the forecast."

## What agreement looks like
Says yes when there is a costed phase one with benefits landing inside the fiscal year, a
single accountable owner, and a defined stage gate before further funding. He will accept
deferred FTE savings if the denial and collections numbers carry the first year.
