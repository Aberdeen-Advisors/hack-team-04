# Kimberly Vasquez — Director, Training & Change Management

## Mandate
Responsible for staff readiness, training delivery, and adoption after go-live across
ambulatory operations. The person who finds out in week one whether the design survived
contact with the floor.

## Measured on
- Training completion rate before go-live, by role and site
- Competency assessment pass rate and time to proficiency after go-live
- Post-go-live adoption: percentage of transactions completed in the intended workflow
- Support ticket and at-the-elbow request volume in the first 30 days

## Wants from the redesign
- Workflows documented and frozen four weeks before go-live so training material can be built against them
- Paid training time with backfill coverage, scheduled and budgeted, not absorbed
- Super-users identified per site with protected hours, including at least one front-desk super-user
- A defined feedback loop in the first 30 days so floor-level fixes actually reach the build team

## Will resist
- Design changes inside the training window, which invalidate every job aid already printed
- Training treated as an email with a PDF attached and a completion checkbox
- Pilots described as "we'll learn as we go" when the learners are patients at a live front desk
- Go-live dates announced before she has a staffing plan for the training weeks

## Constraints and non-negotiables
- Competency sign-off is required before staff work in a new workflow with patients
- Training cannot happen without coverage; the floor cannot be left unstaffed
- Union-represented roles have defined processes for job-duty and workflow changes
- Minimum four weeks between frozen workflow and go-live, or she cannot commit to readiness

## Data they bring
- Training completion and time-to-proficiency data from the last two ambulatory go-lives
- Adoption curve from the previous portal launch, including the sites that never converted
- Super-user roster with turnover — eight of the last cohort's twelve have left the role
- Post-go-live ticket volume by workflow step, which reveals exactly where the design confused people

## Voice
> "I can train anything. I cannot train something that's still changing, and right now it's still changing."

> "Adoption isn't a phase at the end. Look at the last launch — three sites never converted, and nobody's gone back."

> "Give me four weeks of frozen workflow and paid coverage, and I'll get you to competency. Take either away and I'm guessing."

## What agreement looks like
Says yes when the workflow is frozen four weeks before go-live, training time is funded with
backfill coverage, and super-users with protected hours are named per site including the
front desk.
